Isolation and Characterization of Lactic Acid Bacteria

A research-method note based on a published study of sugarcane-juice isolates.

Method at a glance

A five-stage overview of the published workflow. The detailed procedures, assay conditions, calculations and interpretation remain below.

Research objective

To recover lactic acid bacteria (LAB) from sugarcane juice, identify selected isolates, and evaluate their functional, safety and potential probiotic characteristics.

Published experimental workflow

  1. Collection and selective cultivation. Sugarcane juice was collected using aseptic procedures. Samples were serially diluted up to 10−6 and plated on modified MRS agar supplemented with sodium azide, bromocresol purple and sucrose. Plates were incubated aerobically at 37 °C for 48 hours.
  2. Primary screening. Acid-producing colonies were screened by Gram staining, catalase and oxidase tests. Selected isolates were characterized using morphological and biochemical tests.
  3. Molecular identification. The 16S rRNA gene was sequenced using primers 27F and 1492R, and sequences were compared with NCBI records using BLAST.
  4. Cell-free supernatant. Cultures were centrifuged at 10,200 × g for 10 minutes at 4 °C, and supernatants were passed through 0.22 μm filters.
  5. Gastrointestinal tolerance. Growth was evaluated under acidic conditions (pH 3.0 relative to pH 6.5), bile salts (0.3%), and phenol exposure (0.3% and 0.4%). OD600 measurements were used for comparison with controls.
  6. Functional and safety screening. The study assessed aggregation, cell-surface hydrophobicity, antimicrobial and anti-biofilm effects, antioxidant activity, antibiotic susceptibility, hemolysis and DNase activity, alongside food-related applications.

Example calculations

Relative growth (%)

(Growth in test medium / Growth in control medium) × 100

Auto-aggregation (%)

[(A0 − At) / A0] × 100

A0 = initial absorbance; At = absorbance at a later time.

Interpreting the results

Higher relative growth under a stress condition suggests better tolerance under that assay's conditions. Aggregation and hydrophobicity provide indirect indicators of cell-surface behavior; neither alone establishes successful colonization or clinical probiotic efficacy. Safety evaluation requires multiple complementary tests.
