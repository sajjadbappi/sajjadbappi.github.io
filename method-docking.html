Molecular Docking and Dynamics Simulations

A computational workflow illustrated by a published prodigiosin antiviral study.

Source: Rahman, Hossain Bappi & Hossain (2024). This page summarizes methods reported in the publication; it is not a standalone validated software protocol.

Research objective

To investigate predicted interactions of the bacterial pigment prodigiosin with selected dengue and Zika virus protein targets, using molecular docking, binding-pocket analysis, pharmacokinetic predictions and molecular dynamics (MD).

Published computational workflow

  1. Target and ligand preparation. The study prepared viral protein structures and the prodigiosin ligand for computational evaluation, including drug-likeness and ADMET screening.
  2. Binding-pocket evaluation. CASTp 3.0 was used to examine potential protein binding pockets.
  3. Molecular docking. AutoDock Vina v1.1.2 was used with PDBQT-formatted protein and ligand files. The reported docking settings included an exhaustiveness of 8 and a search-space volume of 27,000 Å³.
  4. Interaction visualization. Selected protein–ligand interactions were examined using BIOVIA Discovery Studio 2021.
  5. Molecular dynamics. The best-docked complexes were examined in 100 ns simulations using Desmond. The published study reports an SPC water model, 0.15 M salt, OPLS3e force field, 300 K temperature and approximately 1.01325 bar pressure.
  6. Trajectory analysis. RMSD, RMSF, radius of gyration, solvent-accessible surface area (SASA), and protein–ligand contacts were analyzed to characterize structural behavior.

Understanding the outputs

Docking score
A model-dependent estimate used to compare candidate binding poses; it does not directly measure antiviral effectiveness.
RMSD
Root-mean-square deviation describes structural changes relative to a reference conformation.
RMSF
Root-mean-square fluctuation describes the mobility of individual residues or atoms over the simulation.
Radius of gyration
A measure of structural compactness.
SASA
Solvent-accessible surface area describes the extent of molecular surface exposed to solvent.

Interpretation and limitations

Docking and MD can suggest plausible molecular interactions and relative structural stability, but computational predictions alone do not establish biological efficacy. Experimental validation is necessary.
